# Temporal Causality and Historical Mechanisms

## Time design

Record:

- baseline and preconditions;
- start boundary;
- trigger and critical juncture;
- order of mechanism steps;
- expected lags;
- outcome onset and consolidation;
- feedback and self-reinforcement;
- interruption, reversal, layering, drift, conversion, or replacement;
- the best rival timeline;
- shorter and longer window sensitivity checks.

Do not select the starting date because it makes the focal explanation look correct.

## Continuity protocol

Ancient and contemporary similarity does not establish continuity. A continuity claim requires:

```text
origin -> carrier -> transmission activity -> reproduction or adaptation
-> interruption test -> later outcome
```

The carrier may be an organization, personnel network, law, educational system, archive, ritual, professional practice, political language, or resource relation. Specify who reproduced what and through which activity.

Search equally for:

- discontinuity;
- re-invention;
- independent convergence;
- functional equivalence without genealogy;
- translation that changes the concept;
- survival in one domain but not another.

If meaning or function changes, use terms such as reconstruction, translation, selective inheritance, or functional equivalence instead of unqualified continuity.

## Path dependence gate

Literature anchors: METH-TEMP-001, METH-TEMP-004, CN-METH-001, and CN-METH-002; read their roles and limits in [Method-Evidence Map](method-evidence-map.md) before using the label.

Long duration is not path dependence. Require:

1. an identifiable initial choice set;
2. a critical juncture or branching point;
3. a selected path that was not historically inevitable;
4. a self-reinforcing process such as increasing returns, coordination, learning, adaptive expectations, or power redistribution;
5. rising switching costs or narrowing alternatives;
6. observations that distinguish the path mechanism from repeated functional choice, coercion, or external constraint.

If these cannot be shown, downgrade to a general historical legacy or persistence claim.

Do not merge self-reinforcing path dependence with a reactive sequence. A reactive sequence is a chain in which each event triggers a counterreaction or transformation; it may be temporally dependent without increasing returns, lock-in, or narrowing alternatives. Register it as a distinct temporal mechanism unless the evidence independently establishes self-reinforcement.

## Mechanism syntax

Every step states:

```text
Under context K and input I,
entity N performs activity V,
changing an object from state A to state B or transmitting information,
resources, authority, or constraints,
within time T,
producing output O that enables the next step.
```

Record entity, activity, input, output, changed state, context, timing, productive link, necessity, predicted fingerprint, alternative generator, disconfirming observation, and target source.

For a cross-level step, also record input level, actor level, output level, the aggregation or transmission rule, the bridge evidence, and ecological or atomistic inference risk. Ideas do not become state behavior, and state behavior does not become system-level order, without separately evidenced transitions.

Culture, tradition, institutions, pressure, persuasion, legitimacy, and socialization are labels until this syntax is completed.

## Competing explanations

Include at least one plausible rival and treat it symmetrically. Rivals include:

- a different outcome-generating mechanism;
- a different pathway from the same cause;
- an evidence-generating process such as strategic speech, copied reporting, censorship, or retrospective memory.

The focal and rival mechanisms may be complementary. Supporting the focal mechanism does not eliminate the rival.

## Predicted fingerprints

For each step ask:

- If the step occurred, how likely is this observation?
- Without the step, how likely is the same observation?
- What else could produce it?
- Where, when, and in which source genre should it appear?
- What observation would weaken the step?

Test labels such as straw-in-the-wind, hoop, smoking gun, or doubly decisive summarize an inference; they do not replace source and likelihood analysis.

## Updating and aggregation

Use qualitative Bayesian comparison unless numeric elicitation is transparent and sensitivity-tested. Aggregate only:

```text
observation -> proposition -> step -> mechanism -> bounded claim
```

Evidence moves one level at a time. The mechanism conclusion cannot exceed its weakest necessary step. Dependent evidence counts as one family, not multiple votes.

If evidence inspired the mechanism, mark `evidence-aware discovery` and seek nonoverlapping confirmation. For retrospective analyses, call priors `retrospective prior reconstruction`, not preregistration.

## Counterfactuals

Use a counterfactual only when the antecedent, minimal rewrite, causal pathway, and comparative world are historically possible. Do not treat imagination as evidence. Counterfactual analysis clarifies claims and rivals; it does not substitute for observed process evidence.
