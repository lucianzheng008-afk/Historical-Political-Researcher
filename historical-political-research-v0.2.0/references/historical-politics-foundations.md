# Historical Political Science Foundations

## Purpose

Use this reference to decide whether a task is genuinely historical political science and to translate its Chinese theoretical resources into testable research. Do not use it as a list of conclusions to reproduce.

## Minimum definition

A historical-political-science project must contain all four elements:

1. a political-science problem rather than a history topic alone;
2. a historical dimension that changes the explanation;
3. an analysis of time, sequence, duration, continuity, rupture, or institutional inheritance;
4. feedback from historical materials into a concept, mechanism, scope condition, or theory.

Reject or reframe projects that merely add a historical-background chapter, narrate events chronologically, or attach “历史视野下” to an otherwise unchanged question.

## The supplied Chinese framework

Yang Guangbin's book treats historical political science as a locally grounded disciplinary methodology and emphasizes:

- historical ontology: political orders emerge from different long-run historical structures;
- historical continuity: institutions, political language, behavior, and organization may reproduce effects through time;
- temporal spatialization: present political arrangements are outcomes of diachronic processes;
- theory locality: theories arise from particular historical experiences and may be universalized after their origins are forgotten;
- knowledge production: historical inquiry should produce concepts and explanations rather than only facts;
- theory discrimination: recover the historical conditions hidden by apparently universal theories;
- China as method: use Chinese historical experience to generate questions and concepts rather than treating China only as a case to which external theories are applied;
- world politics: connect political ideas, domestic institutional change, state behavior, and order formation.

Treat these as hypotheses and research prompts. Never encode civilizational labels, continuity, or political legitimacy as self-validating truths.

## Six safeguards

Every claim derived from the framework must pass:

1. **Operationalization:** What properties, carriers, actions, and observations instantiate the claim?
2. **Counterfactual:** What would have happened without the proposed historical condition?
3. **Negative cases:** Where was the condition present without the result, or the result present without the condition?
4. **Rivals:** Which plausible mechanism could produce the same outcome or the same evidence?
5. **Mechanism evidence:** Which entities did what, when, through which transmission, changing what state?
6. **Scope:** In which cases, periods, and conditions should the claim apply or fail?

## Historical ontology profile

Replace civilizational shorthand with a profile of potentially observable historical conditions:

- state-formation path;
- territorial and authority structure;
- social and property structure;
- religious, educational, or ideological organization;
- war, extraction, and fiscal structure;
- bureaucracy and local administration;
- identity and political-community formation;
- position in regional and world orders;
- major shocks, conquest, revolution, and reform;
- institutional carriers through which earlier practices could be reproduced.

The output is a set of testable conditions, not an essence assigned to a country.

## Theory-locality audit

Literature anchors: CN-FIELD-001, CN-FIELD-002, CN-FIELD-003, CN-DEB-001, CN-DEB-003, METH-CONCEPT-001, and METH-SOURCE-002; read their roles and limits in [Method-Evidence Map](method-evidence-map.md) before using the label.

For any imported or established theory, record:

1. when, where, and amid which political conflicts it was developed;
2. its original empirical objects;
3. assumed actors, state forms, social structures, and political purposes;
4. visible and hidden historical conditions;
5. which conditions change when the theory travels;
6. whether the evidence supports retaining, bounding, differentiating, revising, or rejecting the theory.

Historical origin alone never refutes a theory. Judge explanatory performance in the new setting.

## Chinese political research

Separate three questions:

- formation: how the Chinese state and political community were made;
- organization: how authority and political organizations were structured;
- operation: how decisions, implementation, coordination, extraction, and accountability worked.

For every continuity claim, search for rupture, foreign influence, modern reconstruction, institutional carriers, and failed transmission. Terms such as great unity, people-centered rule, consultative practice, civilization, or governance are candidate explanations, not conclusions.

## World-political research

The chain `ideas -> domestic change -> state behavior -> world order` requires a separate test at every transition:

- how ideas entered organizations and policy contests;
- why domestic institutions changed;
- which state behaviors changed and when;
- how multiple behaviors accumulated into regional or world-order effects;
- whether war, capital, technology, security, or organizational politics better explain the same observations.

Do not jump from local political language to national intention or from national intention to system-level outcomes.

## Normative and empirical separation

- Historical origin does not establish legitimacy.
- Social acceptance does not establish moral validity.
- Governance performance does not establish historical continuity.
- Local origin does not establish effectiveness.
- Foreign origin does not establish invalidity.
- A concern with good governance can motivate a question but cannot determine its answer.

For every normative claim, record the normative standard, empirical premises, bridge principle connecting facts to evaluation, and contested values. Historical origin, persistence, popularity, or performance may be empirical premises but cannot themselves serve as the bridge principle.
