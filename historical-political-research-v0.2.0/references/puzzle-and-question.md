# Puzzle and Research Question

## Information sufficiency gate

Before proposing a mature question, decide whether the relevant empirical expectations, literature, cases, and evidence access are known.

Enter targeted retrieval when novelty, current literature, journal expectations, or case facts decide the recommendation. Record:

- source-backed claims;
- inference;
- unknown or needs verification.

If retrieval is unavailable, return provisional question scaffolds and a claim-to-verify list. Never infer “nobody has studied X” from not knowing the literature.

## Topic, problem, puzzle, question, and claim

Literature anchors: METH-CHA-001, EX-IR-001, EX-COMP-001, EX-COMP-002, EX-COMP-005, and EX-COMP-007; read their roles and limits in [Method-Evidence Map](method-evidence-map.md) before using the label.

- **Topic:** a domain such as state building or imperial governance.
- **Problem:** an important unresolved difficulty within that domain.
- **Puzzle:** a supported expectation conflicts with an observation.
- **Question:** asks what explains that conflict within bounded cases and time.
- **Claim:** the evidence-bounded answer.

Do not skip from topic to claim.

## Puzzle generator

Generate candidates from at least three lenses before converging:

1. empirical anomaly: expected A, observed B;
2. similar conditions, different outcomes;
3. different conditions, similar outcomes;
4. temporal anomaly: early, delayed, reversed, or absent change;
5. continuity after revolution, war, reform, or institutional replacement;
6. substantive change beneath formal stability;
7. micro-macro or cross-level mismatch;
8. theory or concept that loses meaning when moved across historical settings.

Write every candidate as:

```text
observation
+ supported prior expectation
+ tension
+ theoretical consequence of resolving it
+ historical materials capable of discriminating among explanations
```

Importance, a new case, a literature gap, Chinese characteristics, or an asserted relationship is not enough.

## Candidate scoring

Score each candidate 1-5 on:

- importance;
- feasibility;
- falsifiability;
- evidence leverage;
- originality;
- value of a negative result;
- historical necessity.

Kill candidates with no beneficiary or theoretical stake, no plausible falsifier, evidence requirements beyond the project, a method in search of a problem, or no reason history changes the inference.

## Historical Political Science Question Card

Require:

- research object and unit;
- spatial and temporal boundaries;
- puzzle and verified expectation;
- defined outcome and threshold;
- focal cause and temporal claim;
- strongest rivals;
- discriminating evidence;
- observations that would weaken or falsify the claim;
- contribution type and level;
- scope conditions;
- case and source feasibility;
- two-week pilot;
- strongest reviewer objection;
- continue, pivot, downgrade, and stop conditions.

A useful form is:

```text
In CASE and PERIOD, why or how did C operate through M to produce or fail to
produce O, rather than the result expected by H2, and what does this imply for
the mechanism, concept, or scope of THEORY?
```

## Contribution ladder

| Level | Contribution | Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | new case, claimed gap, renamed object | insufficient |
| 1 | application of an existing theory | limited |
| 2 | new boundary, scope condition, or heterogeneity | defensible |
| 3 | new or revised mechanism, sequence, or timing effect | strong |
| 4 | concept reconstruction, typology, or theoretical proposition | strong |
| 5 | changes a neighboring field's common understanding | exceptional evidence required |

“Western theory does not fit China” is incomplete. Specify which concept, mechanism, or condition fails and what revised proposition explains more.

## Two-week pilot

The pilot tests whether:

- the puzzle exists;
- the key literature and empirical baseline are accessible;
- at least one bottleneck mechanism step is observable;
- the focal and best rival explanations predict different evidence;
- the required sources can lawfully be accessed;
- the project fits the deadline and word limit.

End with a decision: continue, narrow, redesign, downgrade to a plausibility probe, or stop.
