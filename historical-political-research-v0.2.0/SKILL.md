# Historical Political Research

## Purpose

Turn a topic, call for papers, source collection, proposal, or draft into an auditable historical-political-science research process. The objective is not polished prose alone. It is a defensible chain from puzzle to question, concepts, design, evidence, inference, and review.

Historical materials must change the explanation. A chronology, background section, important topic, alleged literature gap, or label such as “历史视野下” is not enough.

## First response: establish the task contract

Before substantive work, identify or explicitly mark as unknown:

- requested mode and target artifact;
- language, deadline, length, venue, citation style, and audience;
- supplied sources, existing draft, known cases, time range, and search permission;
- whether the user wants exploration, a design, analysis, drafting, or review;
- uncertainty policy and required verification level.

Do not block on every missing detail. Make reversible assumptions, label them, and proceed unless the choice would materially change the project.

Separate domain statements throughout as `source-backed`, `inference`, or `unknown / needs verification`.

Choose the smallest sufficient mode. Do not force every project into process tracing. Route among theory testing, theory building, theory revision, explaining-outcome analysis, comparative historical analysis, concept formation, historical institutional analysis, and nested designs according to the inferential aim.

## Information and fit gates

A project fits historical political science only if it has:

1. a political-science problem;
2. a historical dimension that changes the explanation;
3. analyzable time, sequence, duration, continuity, rupture, or inheritance;
4. feedback from historical evidence into a concept, mechanism, scope condition, or theory.

If evidence is unavailable, return a research design and verification plan. Never invent findings to complete the requested form.

## Mandatory research workflow

For `full_pipeline`, follow this sequence and loop backward when a gate fails:

1. create the task contract and evidence-status rules;
2. map the domain, main explanations, and source terrain;
3. generate multiple puzzle candidates from anomalies, variation, timing, continuity, non-events, micro-macro tensions, or theory migration;
4. rank puzzles for theoretical stakes, surprise, discriminability, evidence access, and feasibility;
5. build the research-question card, including outcome threshold, temporal claim, rivals, falsifiers, scope, pilot, and stop rule;
6. state the smallest defensible contribution and audit novelty;
7. define concepts without anachronism, circularity, or outcome-based thresholds;
8. select the design, periodization, case universe, and comparison protocol;
9. specify entities, activities, state changes, mechanism fingerprints, and rival predictions;
10. run a limited pilot before committing to the full project;
11. collect sources with provenance, access status, independence, and missingness records;
12. connect evidence IDs to claim IDs and update claim states;
13. compare cases or periods using common questions and equivalent evidence layers;
14. cap every conclusion at the weakest necessary design or evidence step;
15. write the title, plan, outline, analysis, or paper requested;
16. run constructive review, hostile review, and a revision loop.

Maintain stable identifiers using the package prefixes: `PUZ-001` (puzzle), `RQ-001` (question), `CON-001` (concept), `H-001` (focal or rival explanation), `CASE-001`, `SRC-001`, `E-001` (evidence), and `CLM-001` (claim). Preserve an identifier across revisions and record the revision separately. Do not merge ledgers by prose similarity.

In `research_plan` mode, do not return a generic reading/search/writing calendar. Before scheduling, state a provisional but explicit outcome threshold, time boundary, case universe, focal explanation, and strongest rival. Include three linked matrices in the answer: `claim or proposition -> source family -> access/dependence risk`, `mechanism step -> predicted fingerprint -> disconfirming observation -> target source`, and `case -> common comparison questions -> evidence-access ceiling`. End with a two-week pilot, dated milestones and deliverables, continue/pivot/stop rules, and the current conclusion ceiling.

## Six non-negotiable safeguards

Every historical explanation must pass:

1. **Operationalization:** identify properties, carriers, activities, and observations.
2. **Counterfactual:** state what should differ without the proposed condition or mechanism.
3. **Negative cases:** search for the cause without the outcome and the outcome without the cause.
4. **Rivals:** formulate the strongest plausible alternative and its observable implications.
5. **Mechanism evidence:** show which entities did what, when, through which transmission, changing what state.
6. **Scope:** define the cases, periods, and conditions where the claim should hold or fail.

Historical origin does not establish legitimacy. Local origin does not establish effectiveness. Foreign origin does not establish invalidity. Keep normative argument and empirical inference separate.

## Hard failure rules

- Do not treat chronology as explanation.
- Do not infer continuity from similar language or function; identify carriers, reproduction, interruption, and transformation.
- Do not claim path dependence without alternatives, a selection event, a reproductive sequence, and durable narrowing or increasing returns.
- Do not define a case universe after choosing confirming cases.
- Do not generalize necessity, sufficiency, prevalence, or average effects from positive cases alone.
- Do not double-count dependent sources.
- Do not treat archival silence as non-occurrence without an evidence-generation audit.
- Do not reuse discovery material as independent confirmation without disclosure.
- Do not project current analytic language onto historical actors without translation.
- Do not fabricate citations, archives, quotations, page numbers, interviews, data, dates, or historical details.

## Artifact protocol

Resolve the absolute directory containing this active `SKILL.md` as `SKILL_DIR`; do not assume the user's current working directory is the Skill repository. Initialize an auditable project with `python3 "$SKILL_DIR/scripts/init_project.py" TARGET`. Keep `TARGET` in the user's intended project location. Use only the needed templates under `assets/templates/`, preserving their column names and stable IDs.

At minimum:

- puzzle work uses `puzzle-register.csv` and `question-cards.md`;
- conceptual work uses `concept-register.csv`;

Run `python3 "$SKILL_DIR/scripts/validate_project.py" PROJECT` before declaring a project structurally complete and `python3 "$SKILL_DIR/scripts/audit_claims.py" PROJECT` before making evidence-backed conclusions.

## Research and delegation

For a material design judgment, use rule -> source -> application -> limit. Distinguish method authority, empirical exemplar, substantive case evidence, and field debate. Metadata supports metadata claims; abstracts support only their stated claims; exact page claims require inspecting the cited edition and page.

When tools and agents are available, parallelize only independent tasks such as literature mapping, rival-theory construction, case-universe checks, source audits, and blind review. Assign explicit deliverables and evidence standards. Integrate all outputs into one ledger. Agreement among agents is not corroboration, and agent output is not a source.

Optional adapters may assist without replacing this workflow:

- `academic-search` for verifiable literature discovery;
- `good-question` for question pressure tests;
- `process-tracing-research` for deeper within-case causal inference;
- `social-science-paper-writing` for manuscript alignment;
- `advantage-led-academic-writing` or `good-story` only after question and evidence gates pass.

## Output standard

Lead with the research judgment, then show evidence status, mechanism or design logic, rivals, limitations, and next action. Use `PASS`, `PARTIAL`, and `FAIL` in reviews and attach a repair and verification test to every material failure.

If the evidence cannot decide, say so. A bounded unresolved conclusion is a successful research result; invented certainty is not.
